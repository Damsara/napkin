> The four kinds of alternative spar generates, each with its generating prompt and the trade-off it characteristically carries.

# Alternative kinds

Every alternative is written in this shape, at most 200 words:

```
**Name**:
**Pitch**: one line.
**Hero feature**: what a sixty-second demo shows.
**For**: the person, if different from the original's.
**Four forces**: push, pull, anxiety, habit for that person; the weakest force named.
**Gives up**: what this alternative loses versus the original.
**Well or puddle**: and why.
```

An alternative with no hero feature is not an alternative. An alternative that gives up nothing has not been thought through.

## Wedge
*Prompt*: Find the smallest, most concentrated group inside the original's audience for whom the struggling moment is sharpest, served by no one today, and rebuild the product for them alone. Name the path from that group to the next one.
*Characteristic trade-off*: a smaller initial market and a slower path to breadth, in exchange for a person who wants it right now.

## Circumstance
*Prompt*: Keep the job, change the circumstance or the person. Who else hires something for this job, in a different situation, with a different alternative today? Rebuild the product for that hire.
*Characteristic trade-off*: the original person is no longer served; the builder may know the new person less well.

## Underdo
*Prompt*: Take the original and remove everything except the one capability that does the job. Make that one thing complete, fast, and obvious. Name what the incumbents do that this deliberately refuses to do.
*Characteristic trade-off*: table stakes the person may expect are absent; the product wins only if the one thing is enough to switch for.

## Reframe
*Prompt*: Name the assumption the original rests on that is most likely wrong (about the person, the job, the alternative, or why they have not solved it already). Assume it is wrong and describe the product that follows.
*Characteristic trade-off*: it depends on the reframe being right, which the riskiest-assumption test must check before anything is built.

## Keep the original
Added by the chair, never generated. Written in the same shape so it competes on the same terms. It must be possible for the original to win.
