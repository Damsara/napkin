> The sixteen words napkin thinks with. Each is a concept the model already knows; the definition here fixes which sense napkin means.

# Leading words

## job story
*Klement, JTBD.* "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]." The situation replaces the persona: it names the trigger, not the demographic. Every feature in the spec is written as one.

## struggling moment
*Moesta.* The concrete last time the person thought "maybe I can do better." No struggle, no demand. A struggling moment is a past event with a time and a place; "people are frustrated with X" is not one.

## four forces
*Moesta.* Push of the situation, pull of the new solution, anxiety about the new, habit of the present. A person switches only when push + pull exceeds anxiety + habit. Builders over-invest in pull and ignore the two restraining forces; the verdict names the weakest force.

## well or puddle
*Paul Graham.* A well is something a small number of people want a great deal; a puddle is something a large number want a little. Choose the well, and check there is a path out of it.

## wedge
*Thiel, Graham.* A small group, concentrated together, served by few or no competitors, that the product can dominate first, with adjacent groups reachable afterwards. "Always err on the side of starting too small."

## appetite
*Shape Up.* A fixed amount of time set before design begins; scope varies to fit it. The reverse of an estimate. napkin sets it in round one and enforces it in shape. Solo builders usually choose two to six weeks.

## epicenter
*Getting Real.* The thing the product cannot live without. Build it completely before anything else; remove it and the product makes no sense. napkin's hero feature is the epicenter.

## skateboard
*Kniberg.* The earliest usable whole slice through the underlying need, never a component of the final thing. A wheel is not testable; a skateboard is. The hero feature ships as a skateboard.

## delighter and must-be
*Kano.* A must-be is expected and unnoticed when present; a delighter is unexpected and loved. Delighters become must-bes over time. The hero is a delighter; must-bes are the floor and cannot be the reason to switch.

## rabbit hole
*Shape Up.* A place where the build could sink: novel technical work, an integration nobody has verified, a design problem with no known solution, a hard decision left open. Walk the use case in slow motion to find them; name them in the spec.

## no-go
*Shape Up.* Something explicitly out of bounds for this appetite. Declared, not implied.

## riskiest assumption
*Higham, Bland.* Of everything that must be true for the product to work, the one that is most important and least evidenced. Its test is the smallest experiment that could prove it wrong, with a number that means pass. The test comes before the build and may involve no code.

## pre-mortem
*Klein.* "It is a year from now and this failed completely. Why?" Written independently before any discussion. Prospective hindsight raises the count of reasons found by about a third. napkin's Skeptic seat runs it because a solo builder has no dissenting team.

## competitive alternative
*Dunford.* What the target person would do if the product did not exist: a spreadsheet, a script, an intern, doing nothing, an incumbent. Positioning starts here; starting anywhere else produces positioning that sounds good in the office and fails outside it.

## trust circle
*Rachitsky's first-ten-customers data.* Audiences ordered by how much they already trust the builder: personal network matching the job story first, then the exact community where the target person gathers, then press or a directory launch. Press amplifies momentum; it does not create it.

## launch series
*Linear.* Launches compound. Plan three, each aimed at one audience, each different from the last, rather than one event.
