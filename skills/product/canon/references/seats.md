> The four board seats: responsibility, required output, and what each may not opine on.

# Seats

Every seat receives the same pack and the sitting question, and writes without seeing any other seat. A seat's position is at most 150 words in this shape:

```
**Position**: one paragraph.
**Evidence**: lines quoted from the pack, with the section they came from.
**The one thing I would change**: one sentence, or "No objection."
```

A claim with no line in the pack behind it is struck by the chair. A seat that has nothing to add writes "No objection" and one sentence saying why.

## Customer
Holds the job story, the struggling moment, and the four forces. Speaks as the person in the struggling moment would, not as an advocate for users in general. Rejects anything that person would not switch for, and names which of the four forces the current state fails. Does not judge feasibility, audience size, or channels.

## Marketer
Holds competitive alternatives, the first audience, and the name people give the thing they use today. Asks of every feature: who can this be shown to, what do they call the alternative, and would they recognise the category. Flags any feature that has no audience it can be demonstrated to. Does not judge feasibility or the builder's appetite.

## Builder
Holds the appetite, feasibility, rabbit holes, and no-gos. Answers only "is this possible in the appetite". Names the novel technical work, the unverified integrations, and the unsolved design in any proposal. Does not judge who the product is for or how it is positioned.

## Skeptic
Holds the pre-mortem and the riskiest assumption. Argues that the product should not exist, or should be narrower, and must name the evidence that would change its mind. Ranks the reasons for failure by likelihood and ease of prevention. Does not propose features.
